# ai-tells-extended.md: the 200-source deep-research sweep

ai-tells.md is the curated, load-bearing ban list — short, opinionated, checked in preflight.md. This file is its evidence base: a much larger, less-curated catalog pulled from a dedicated research pass across ~150 sources (GitHub anti-slop rulesets, design-critique blogs, academic code-smell papers, accessibility audits, Hacker News/Reddit threads, Habr) specifically to maximize the number of distinct, checkable "this looks/reads/is-built like AI made it" signals in one place. Expect overlap with ai-tells.md and ai-code-smells.md — that's reinforcement from independent sources, not a bug. When in doubt whether something is banned: if it is named here, it is banned by the same standard as everything in ai-tells.md (register-gated, HUMAN judgment call to override, never a scanner).

**Use it as a checklist, not a novel.** Skim category headers for what's relevant to the surface at hand; visual-forensics.md's pixel sweep should walk this file too when the ban 1-50 catalog in ai-tells.md comes up clean but the page still smells generic.

## Visual & interaction tells

1. **Purple/indigo/blue gradient default** — A blue-to-purple, indigo-to-violet, or purple-to-cyan gradient used as the hero background, CTA button fill, or primary accent with no brand rationale — the single most cited AI-generated-UI color signature, traced to Tailwind's `indigo-500` saturating training data. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills)
2. **Unmodified Tailwind/shadcn default color tokens** — Stock palette tokens used as-is (`indigo-500`, `slate-900`, `emerald-400`, `violet-*`) or the exact hex `#4F46E5`/`#5E6AD2`, or out-of-the-box shadcn/ui styling, instead of a customized palette. (https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills; https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh)
3. **Excessive glassmorphism** — Blur/backdrop-filter frosted panels applied simultaneously across navbar, cards, modals, and sidebar (or layered over a gradient blob) rather than as an accent on one element. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills)
4. **Aurora/mesh-blob/floating-orb background** — Soft blurred organic color blobs, mesh gradients, or generic 3D floating orbs/spheres used as ambient decoration behind hero or section content with no semantic role. (https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills; https://dev.to/jaainil/ai-purple-problem-make-your-ui-unmistakable-3ono)
5. **Radial-gradient / spotlight glow behind content** — A saturated radial glow or faint accent haze placed behind hero/section content on a dark page as a generic ambient lighting shortcut, not tied to any real material or focus state. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
6. **Glow effects stacked everywhere** — Colored glow/box-shadow effects applied simultaneously to cards, buttons, icons, badges, backgrounds, and borders instead of reserved as an accent on 1-2 elements. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui)
7. **Dark mode with unprompted neon/purple glow** — A dark background paired with saturated neon or purple glow accents on headings, buttons, or borders that nobody asked for; independently converged on by multiple different models. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://www.claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui)
8. **Background grid / blueprint pattern** — Grid squares, blueprint lines, or graph-paper lines used as a decorative page background with no connection to product identity or an actual canvas/measurement task. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://impeccable.style/slop/)
9. **Trend-stacking decoration** — Multiple decorative trends combined at once with no purpose (glassmorphism + mesh gradient + glow + monospace + grid + rounded UI all appearing together). (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop)
10. **Dark mode default for no reason** — The entire page is dark simply because it "looks tech," with no branding or product-type justification. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop)
11. **Too many colors in palette** — 5-7+ distinct colors used on one page with no coherent system, instead of a palette capped at 2-3 core colors plus 1 accent. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop)
12. **Neon saturation overload** — Multiple competing saturated colors (5-6) fighting for attention with no color hierarchy establishing what matters most. (https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui)
13. **Timid, evenly-distributed color palette** — Colors spread evenly with no dominant hue and no sharp accent, and no evidence of complementary/analogous/triadic color theory, instead of committing to one dominant color with a bold accent. (https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website; https://docs.bswen.com/blog/2026-03-20-ai-generated-ui-anti-patterns/)
14. **Single accent color overused everywhere** — One accent color applied to buttons, icons, badges, links, dividers, backgrounds, and glows all at once instead of sparingly at one key moment. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop)
15. **Rainbow accent bars / status colors with no logic** — A colored left-border stripe on list items that cycles through the palette with no meaning tied to category/priority, or every status badge (including neutral ones) getting a different color instead of grey. (https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui; https://dev.to/kiwibreaksme/i-catalogued-every-tell-that-makes-a-ui-look-ai-generated-my-own-tool-kept-failing-the-test-n52)
16. **Meaningless decorative status dots** — Small colored dots that look like they should indicate live state (online/offline, success/error) but appear uniformly with no corresponding real status. (https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui)
17. **HSL instead of OKLCH color model** — UI colors defined in HSL rather than the more perceptually-uniform OKLCH, producing less intentional-looking palettes. (https://github.com/shannhk/avoid-slop)
18. **Pure, hue-less gray neutrals** — Backgrounds/borders/text use true zero-chroma gray (e.g. #808080-style) instead of a designed neutral. (https://github.com/shannhk/avoid-slop)
19. **Neutrals with no brand-hue tint** — Gray/neutral tones lack a small amount of the brand hue's chroma mixed in, making them look generic and disconnected from the rest of the palette. (https://github.com/shannhk/avoid-slop)
20. **Cream/beige palette as "safe tasteful" default** — A warm cream or beige page background (#faf8f5, bg-stone-50) used reflexively as the safe alternative to the purple-gradient default. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://www.claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui)
21. **"Tasteful default" combo: cream + serif + sage** — Warm cream background, a serif display heading font (Instrument Serif, Fraunces, Playfair), and a sage/forest-green primary color appearing together — a newer but equally recognizable AI template. (https://www.claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui; https://github.com/JCarterJohnson/vibecoded-design-tells)
22. **Overcorrection into dated beige/serif-everywhere** — Attempts to avoid looking generic swing too far into paper-like beige backgrounds and serif-everywhere typography, reading as outdated/governmental rather than deliberately designed. (https://dev.to/kiwibreaksme/i-catalogued-every-tell-that-makes-a-ui-look-ai-generated-my-own-tool-kept-failing-the-test-n52)
23. **Pure black text (#000)** — Body copy set in literal pure black instead of a softened dark grey, producing harsher-than-intended contrast. (https://dev.to/kiwibreaksme/i-catalogued-every-tell-that-makes-a-ui-look-ai-generated-my-own-tool-kept-failing-the-test-n52)
24. **Pure black/white surface fills** — Literal `#000000` or `#ffffff` used for backgrounds/surfaces instead of a hue-tinted neutral, reading as flat and synthetic. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)
25. **Flat white/light-gray background, no atmosphere** — Page background defaults to plain white or a "safe" subtle gray with no layered gradients, texture, geometric pattern, or depth. (https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p; https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)
26. **Sterile default (over-corrected slop)** — Flat near-white background, thin grey borders, small radius, no texture, generic font, no identity — the "safe void" left after stripping slop without adding direction. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop)
27. **VibeCode purple / moody desaturated purple (model collapse)** — A specific muted purple/indigo tone recurs as the default accent across generations, an artifact of generative model collapse narrowing toward one "safe average" color. (https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/; https://dev.to/jaainil/ai-purple-problem-make-your-ui-unmistakable-3ono)
28. **Perma dark mode with washed-out text** — Dark theme applied by default using medium-grey body text and all-caps section labels, giving a flat, low-effort dark UI. (https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)

## Shape, Radius & Depth

29. **Uniform rounded corners on everything** — Every card, button, input, and container uses the same border-radius (often `rounded-2xl`/pill) with no variation for hierarchy or component type. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills)
30. **Extreme border-radius turning cards into blobs** — Cards/sections/inputs over-rounded to 24px+ (e.g. 44px) instead of topping out around 12-16px, with full-pill reserved for tags/buttons. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
31. **Overly soft, uniform shadows on everything** — Large, soft box-shadows applied to every component so the whole page looks like it's floating, instead of using shadow selectively to mark elevation. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop)
32. **Uniform, low-opacity (~0.1) box-shadows** — Cards/panels get the same faint drop shadow at a suspiciously uniform low opacity, giving flat, homogenized depth instead of a considered elevation system. (https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website; https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p)
33. **Hairline border + wide diffuse shadow ("ghost card")** — A 1px hairline border combined with a wide, soft drop shadow on the same element, committing to neither a defined edge nor soft elevation. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
34. **Flat, unlayered/single-utility shadows** — Shadows use one flat utility (e.g. `shadow-md`) with no color tinting or multi-layer depth, giving a cheap/plastic look instead of a crafted elevation system. (https://habr.com/ru/articles/1004960/)
35. **Uniform visual depth / no elevation hierarchy** — All elements sit on the same visual z-plane (cards, modals, headers all equally flat), with no layered elevation system. (https://habr.com/ru/articles/1004960/)
36. **Colored side-border/stripe on a rounded card** — A thick colored border/stripe on one edge (usually left) of a rounded card, clashing with its border-radius; called one of the single most recognizable AI-UI tells and specifically flagged as common in Claude Code output. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)
37. **Repeating-gradient stripe texture** — Repeating CSS gradient stripes used as surface decoration/texture rather than a deliberate design choice. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
38. **Bento-grid layouts** — A grid of variously sized rectangular tiles used to showcase features, imitating Apple-style marketing pages regardless of fit. (https://github.com/JCarterJohnson/vibecoded-design-tells)
39. **Card-in-card nesting** — A bordered container holding another visually near-identical card (sometimes a further "micro-card") with no semantic reason for the extra containment layer. (https://alexlavaee.me/blog/lessons-learned-designing-with-ai/; https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)

## Typography

40. **Inter/Roboto/Geist/Arial as the sole font** — The entire site uses one of these ubiquitous "safe" sans fonts for both headings and body with no secondary/display face or hierarchy, because they dominate training-data tutorials. (https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills; https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)
41. **Impeccable.style-cited overused fonts (Inter/Geist/Space Grotesk/Instrument Serif)** — This specific quartet is called out as so common in AI-generated UI that using any of them unpaired is an instant tell. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)
42. **Convergent "distinctive" font even under explicit instruction** — Even when told to avoid generic fonts, models still converge repeatedly on the same "distinctive" alternative (e.g. Space Grotesk) across separate generations. (https://www.vibe0.com.au/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-website-is-vibe-coded)
43. **No typography pairing / single font for everything** — Only one font family used across headings, body, labels, and buttons, with size as the sole hierarchy signal instead of intentional serif/sans pairing or weight contrast. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)
44. **Flat type hierarchy (heading/body sizes too close)** — Heading, subheading, and body font sizes sit within less than a ~1.25 ratio of each other, producing no clear visual hierarchy. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://rovela.ai/blog/do-ai-websites-look-generic)
45. **No visual hierarchy beyond font size** — The only signal of importance is "bigger text = header," with no use of weight, color, spacing, or layout for emphasis. (https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website; https://docs.bswen.com/blog/2026-03-20-ai-generated-ui-anti-patterns/)
46. **Weak/default typography (line length, size, spacing)** — Default system fonts combined with awkward line lengths, inconsistent heading sizes across sections, and cramped line-height, indicating type was never deliberately set. (https://www.sinton.agency/blog/how-to-spot-a-vibe-coded-website; https://vibe-eval.com/ai-security/vibe-coding-vulnerabilities/)
47. **Broken scale and hierarchy (competing sizes)** — Logos, headings, and images sized so they compete rather than guide the eye, with no clear hierarchy of importance. (https://www.sinton.agency/blog/how-to-spot-a-vibe-coded-website; https://vibe-eval.com/ai-security/vibe-coding-vulnerabilities/)
48. **Italic serif display headline** — An oversized italic serif used as the primary hero headline; started as a tasteful choice but became the universal AI-startup hero pattern. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
49. **Single italic-serif accent word in an otherwise sans headline** — One word inside an all-Inter hero headline is set in italic serif as a faux-editorial accent. (https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)
50. **Oversized hero headline dominating the viewport** — A full-sentence headline set at display size fills the entire fold, with long headlines blown up too large. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
51. **Crushed letter-spacing on display type** — Letter spacing pulled so tight that characters lose their own shapes, hurting legibility. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
52. **Wide letter-spacing on body text** — Letter-spacing above ~0.05em applied to body paragraphs (not just short uppercase labels), disrupting character grouping and slowing reading. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
53. **Generic AI monospace/uppercase-tracked typography** — Large monospace headings and uppercase section labels with wide letter tracking (e.g. "HOW IT WORKS") used purely for a "techy" look with no brand rationale. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop)
54. **Dense, tiny console-font typography** — Pages cram large amounts of small monospace/console-style text into every available inch, hurting readability and scannability. (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47864393)
55. **Extreme type sizing (tiny body on large desktop viewports)** — Body copy set at ~14px on large desktop screens, ignoring the actual viewport size. (https://dev.to/kiwibreaksme/i-catalogued-every-tell-that-makes-a-ui-look-ai-generated-my-own-tool-kept-failing-the-test-n52)
56. **Title Case headings and colon-subtitle structure** — Headings rendered in Title Case, and titles built as "Main Title: Descriptive Subtitle," instead of sentence-case, direct headings. (https://github.com/alexconner-79/ill-communication)
57. **Missing tabular-nums on numeric columns** — Lists of prices, dates, or metrics fail to align vertically because proportional figures are used instead of `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)
58. **Gradient-filled text** — Headings or metrics rendered with a CSS gradient fill (`background-clip: text`) instead of a solid color, hurting scannability and reading as decorative rather than meaningful. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p)

## Layout & Composition

59. **Centered hero + one or two CTA buttons** — Hero is center-aligned text (headline + subheadline) with a single CTA, or two buttons side-by-side ("Get Started" + "Learn More"), repeated near-identically across generated pages. (https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills; https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)
60. **Full-viewport centered hero ("the default LLM landing page")** — `min-height:100vh` hero with everything centered, one short sentence, and one big CTA. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)
61. **Hero eyebrow/pill chip above oversized headline** — A tiny uppercase letter-spaced label or pill-chip sitting immediately above an oversized hero headline — the default AI SaaS hero composition. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)
62. **Badge/pill directly above the H1 ("New," "v2.0," "AI Powered")** — A small pill or capsule badge with a thin border, glow, and small dot, in uppercase text, placed above the hero headline as decoration rather than a real functional status. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)
63. **Text-left / visual-right hero with two pill CTAs** — Product hero defaults to text on one side and an image/illustration on the other, paired with two pill-shaped CTA buttons. (https://dev.to/kiwibreaksme/i-catalogued-every-tell-that-makes-a-ui-look-ai-generated-my-own-tool-kept-failing-the-test-n52)
64. **Three-column feature card grid** — Feature sections rendered as exactly three equal-width cards, each with an icon, heading, and short body copy — the ubiquitous template structure reproduced regardless of product. (https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills; https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh)
65. **Icon-tile stacked above a feature heading** — A small rounded-square icon container (often in a pastel-tinted chip) placed above each feature heading — the universal AI feature-card template. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://dev.to/kiwibreaksme/i-catalogued-every-tell-that-makes-a-ui-look-ai-generated-my-own-tool-kept-failing-the-test-n52)
66. **Identical/copy-paste feature cards** — All feature cards share identical size, height, icon placement, and padding with no visual variation reflecting content hierarchy. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://alexlavaee.me/blog/lessons-learned-designing-with-ai/)
67. **"Ghost index numbers" replacing icon chips** — Decorative index notation (01 · 02 · 03) used in place of icon chips to introduce sections. (https://dev.to/kiwibreaksme/i-catalogued-every-tell-that-makes-a-ui-look-ai-generated-my-own-tool-kept-failing-the-test-n52)
68. **Eyebrow/index label above every section heading** — Every section prefixed with an uppercase mono-cap label like "01 / EXAMPLES," worst as a two-column "tag-left, heading-right" hanging-header layout — called the single most reliable AI-templated tell for editorial-style SaaS pages. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)
69. **Numbered 1-2-3 step sequences** — "How it works" sections rendered as three numbered steps in a row, a generic explanatory pattern. (https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)
70. **Stat/KPI banner rows** — A horizontal row of large numbers with small captions (e.g. "10k+ users"), or a hero metric layout (big number + label + supporting stats + gradient accent), used as a generic credibility section. (https://impeccable.style/slop/; https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)
71. **Uppercase-overline + big-number metric cards** — KPI sections repeat the identical formula of a small uppercase label above a large number across every card. (https://dev.to/kiwibreaksme/i-catalogued-every-tell-that-makes-a-ui-look-ai-generated-my-own-tool-kept-failing-the-test-n52)
72. **Middle-tier pricing plan artificially elevated** — In a three-tier pricing section, the middle plan is visually elevated/highlighted (larger, raised, or bordered) as the default "recommended" treatment regardless of whether that's the right plan to push. (https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh; https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)
73. **Centered, vertically stacked full-width sections** — Every section is full-width and center-aligned, stacked vertically with no asymmetry, overlap, or grid-breaking element — the layout equivalent of "beige carpet." (https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh; https://cloud.tencent.com/developer/article/2687093)
74. **Uniform section rhythm/composition repeated identically** — Every section uses the same padding/margin/gap and the same composition (centered title + subtitle + identical card grid) with no variation between sections. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://rovela.ai/blog/do-ai-websites-look-generic)
75. **Alternating section background as the only variation** — Sections are distinguished from one another solely by flipping the background color every other section, with no other compositional change. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop)
76. **Inconsistent/arbitrary spacing** — Margins and padding vary unevenly between sections and elements (mixing `p-3`, `p-7`, arbitrary `mt-[37px]`) with no coherent spacing scale, breaking the page's rhythm. (https://www.sinton.agency/blog/how-to-spot-a-vibe-coded-website; https://vibe-eval.com/ai-security/vibe-coding-vulnerabilities/)
77. **Perfectly rigid 8-point-grid spacing with no contrast** — Layout uses only symmetrical spacing from the 8-point scale everywhere with no intentional scale contrast or asymmetry. (https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills)
78. **Misalignment ("almost" grid)** — Elements that almost line up but don't — off-by-a-few-pixels edges, inconsistent column gutters — instead of aligning to a consistent grid. (https://www.claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui)
79. **No information hierarchy across sections** — Every section is rendered with the same visual weight, with nothing leading the eye or indicating what to see first. (https://www.claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui)
80. **Centered-everything / endless whitespace** — Huge vertical padding with every element centered and no variation in alignment, producing an empty, generic feel. (https://www.claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui)
81. **Perfect center alignment throughout** — Everything is symmetrically centered with no intentional asymmetry or unbalanced composition. (https://cloud.tencent.com/developer/article/2687093)
82. **Text overflow/clipping** — A heading or label runs past or behind its container, or a fixed-width card breaks with long content, from placing text without testing real string lengths. (https://www.claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui)
83. **Cards flush against the scroller edge** — Horizontally scrolling cards lose their edge inset because the containing panel lacks a matching gutter, so one edge sits flush against the viewport/scroller boundary. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
84. **Text occluded by an overlapping element** — An opaque layer (image, panel, badge) visually covers readable text underneath it. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
85. **Unbalanced multi-column height** — In a multi-column layout, one opening column runs much taller than its neighbor, leaving large dead/empty space. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
86. **Heading crowded against the wrong block** — A heading sits closer to the content above it than to its own following content, inverting expected proximity/grouping. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
87. **Cramped internal padding** — Text sits with only a couple pixels of padding inside a bordered/colored container instead of at least 8-16px. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
88. **Body text flush against viewport edge** — Body paragraphs render with no container padding at all, touching the left or right edge of the viewport. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
89. **Inconsistent styling across repeated instances** — Padding, margins, and visual details differ between instances of what should be identical UI elements (e.g. a strikethrough rendered differently in two places). (https://habr.com/ru/companies/surfstudio/articles/951660/)
90. **Shaky/untested responsive layouts** — Resizing the browser or viewing on a phone reveals layouts that break, overlap, or overflow at screen sizes the AI was never explicitly prompted to handle. (https://solodesign.cc/blog/ai-design-slop-the-tells/)
91. **Generic AI nav bar shape** — Wordmark hard-left, 4-5 inline text links, a CTA button hard-right, full width, sticky, white background with a 1px hairline bottom border — a genre-blind generic SaaS nav applied to any type of site. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)
92. **Generic AI footer shape** — Four link columns (Product/Company/Resources/Legal), a social-icon row, a copyright line, faint top border, neutral grey background — identical across thousands of generated pages. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)
93. **Editorial "specimen sheet" drift** — The model defaults to a foundry/editorial layout (numbered left-margin labels, huge serif display, asymmetric spans, hairline rules, typographic-only CTA) even when the brief never asked for that energy. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)
94. **Safe, predictable section ordering** — Pages follow a formulaic, low-risk sequence (hero → features → cards → CTA) with uniform, cautious spacing rather than a deliberate, brand-specific structure. (https://unpromptable.substack.com/p/5-ai-website-design-tips-for-websites)
95. **Skeleton/placeholder used as the "product screenshot"** — Grey placeholder bars or skeleton-loading blocks used as the actual product image in a hero or feature section instead of a real screenshot. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop)
96. **Re-drawn fake UI chrome** — Hand-built fake browser bars (URL pill + traffic-light dots), phone frames, or code-block/IDE window chrome wrapping a screenshot, recognizable because details are subtly wrong (dots aren't real, notch shape is off) and redundant with the user's real environment. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)

## Icons, Illustration & Imagery

## Accessibility-Adjacent Visual Tells

130. **Focus outline removed with no visible replacement** — Interactive elements strip the default focus ring via CSS (`outline:none`) without substituting a `:focus-visible` style, leaving keyboard users with no visible focus indicator. ## Accessibility tells

**1. Broken mobile layout** — Horizontal overflow, clipped/colliding cards or text, broken navbar at mobile breakpoints, mobile treated as an afterthought. [github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop, github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop]

**2. Undersized touch targets** — Interactive elements sized below the ~44×44px minimum, and layouts that don't reflow properly for touch on small screens. [mintviz.usv.ro/publications/2025.W4A.3.pdf, dev.to/kaplich/i-analyzed-100-vibe-coded-websites-and-found-these-common-mistakes-5275]

**3. Poor/low text contrast (general)** — Light grey/gray text on a similar-toned or gradient background, failing WCAG AA (4.5:1 normal, 3:1 large text) despite looking fine in isolated spots. [github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop, impeccable.style/slop/, github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop, www.sinton.agency/blog/how-to-spot-a-vibe-coded-website, vibe-eval.com/ai-security/vibe-coding-vulnerabilities/, variantsystems.io/blog/vibe-code-anti-patterns, solodesign.cc/blog/ai-design-slop-the-tells/, newsletters.ai/p/ai-vs-purple, blog.getdiffer.com/design-tips-vibe-coded-project, habr.com/ru/companies/surfstudio/articles/951660/, smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop]

**4. Contrast failures concentrated in placeholder/disabled/colored-background text** — The single most common axe-core-flagged WCAG failure specifically clusters in placeholder text, disabled states, and text over colored/gradient backgrounds. [vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design, frontendmasters.com/blog/ai-generated-ui-is-inaccessible-by-default/]

**5. Gradient text contrast failure** — Gradient-filled headline/accent text fails WCAG contrast because only some points along the gradient are checked (or none are). [dev.to/jaainil/ai-purple-problem-make-your-ui-unmistakable-3ono]

**6. Dark-mode text contrast issues** — Dark-theme body text sits right at the edge of WCAG minimums, or uses dark-brown/beige/dark-grey-on-dark-grey combinations that fall well below ~4:1. [www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/, news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47864393]

**7. Unreadable low-contrast form input text** — Form fields styled with such low text/background contrast that typed input is illegible even to users with normal vision. [news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47864393]

**8. Not keyboard navigable** — Interactive elements only operate via mouse: no logical tab order, no Enter/Space activation, no Escape-to-close, no visible focus indicator; the single worst-scoring criterion in expert audits (severity 3.8/4, ~80% violation rate absent explicit accessibility prompting). [dev.to/kaplich/i-analyzed-100-vibe-coded-websites-and-found-these-common-mistakes-5275, blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/, mintviz.usv.ro/publications/2025.W4A.3.pdf, www.sinton.agency/blog/how-to-spot-a-vibe-coded-website, vibe-eval.com/ai-security/vibe-coding-vulnerabilities/, variantsystems.io/blog/vibe-code-anti-patterns, solodesign.cc/blog/ai-design-slop-the-tells/, newsletters.ai/p/ai-vs-purple, blog.getdiffer.com/design-tips-vibe-coded-project]

Skipped or non-sequential heading levels** — Heading hierarchy jumps (e.g. h1→h3, or h4 directly after h2) instead of stepping through each level, breaking the document outline screen readers rely on. [impeccable.style/slop/, pedalpoint.com/2026/05/ai-generated-code-is-inaccessible-by-default/, blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/]

**17. Missing expanded/collapsed state on toggles** — Disclosure/accordion/menu triggers rotate a chevron purely via CSS transform but never set `aria-expanded`, so the open/closed state is invisible to assistive tech. [vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design, www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it, frontendmasters.com/blog/ai-generated-ui-is-inaccessible-by-default/, pedalpoint.com/2026/05/ai-generated-code-is-inaccessible-by-default/]

**18. Missing aria-controls linkage** — A toggle button has no `aria-controls` pointing at the panel it opens, so the trigger-to-content relationship is only visual, never programmatic. [vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design, www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it, frontendmasters.com/blog/ai-generated-ui-is-inaccessible-by-default/]

**19. No keyboard handling on custom widgets** — Custom interactive elements have `onClick` but no `onKeyDown`, so even a focused element does nothing on Enter/Space, and composite widgets lack arrow-key navigation per WAI-ARIA patterns. [vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design, www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it, frontendmasters.com/blog/ai-generated-ui-is-inaccessible-by-default/]

**20. The Invisible Modal** — Modals built from nested `<div>`s missing `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, an accessible label, a focus trap, an Escape handler, and focus restoration to the trigger on close. [vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design, www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it, frontendmasters.com/blog/ai-generated-ui-is-inaccessible-by-default/, blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/]

**21. Custom select/listbox reinvented with divs** — A dropdown built from clickable `<div>`s missing `role="listbox"`/`option`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-selected`, `aria-activedescendant`, and keyboard/arrow-key navigation, instead of `<select>` or a proper headless Listbox. [vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design, www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it, frontendmasters.com/blog/ai-generated-ui-is-inaccessible-by-default/]

**22. Inconsistent motion-reduction guarding** — `motion-safe:`/`motion-reduce:` (or `prefers-reduced-motion`) variants are applied to some transitions (e.g. a chevron rotation) but omitted from other transform/position animations (sliding panels, scaling) in the same component. [vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design, www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it, frontendmasters.com/blog/ai-generated-ui-is-inaccessible-by-default/]

**23. Missing prefers-reduced-motion support entirely** — Animations/transitions ship with no `prefers-reduced-motion` guard at all, and generated pages more broadly ignore OS-level motion/contrast/dark-mode preferences. [www.claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui, pedalpoint.com/2026/05/ai-generated-code-is-inaccessible-by-default/, blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/, github.com/Vanszs/Anti-AI-UI, github.com/wilwaldon/Claude-Code-Frontend-Design-Toolkit]

**24. Removed or missing visible focus indicator** — Default focus rings are stripped (e.g. `outline:none`) without a `:focus-visible` replacement, and buttons/links generally lack distinct hover/focus/active states, leaving keyboard users unable to see where focus is. [vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design, www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it, frontendmasters.com/blog/ai-generated-ui-is-inaccessible-by-default/, smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop, habr.com/ru/articles/1004960/]

**25. No programmatic focus management on dynamic content** — Elements that appear dynamically (modals, sidebars) don't receive programmatic focus when toggled into view, stranding keyboard/screen-reader users. [blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/]

**26. No required-field indicators** — Form fields give no visual marker (asterisk, label text) showing which fields are mandatory. [prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website, www.vibe0.com.au/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-website-is-vibe-coded, news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46532362]

**27. Forms with no validation/error feedback** — Forms look structurally correct (labels, inputs, submit button) but give no inline validation, error highlighting, or success feedback, since the model trained on static form markup rather than interactive failure states. [github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills, docs.bswen.com/blog/2026-03-20-ai-generated-ui-anti-patterns/, www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide]

**28. Form inputs without associated labels** — Input fields generated without a `<label>` or accessible name, leaving screen reader users with no indication of the field's purpose. [pedalpoint.com/2026/05/ai-generated-code-is-inaccessible-by-default/]

**29. Orphaned/unclear form error messages** — Validation error text isn't programmatically linked to its input (no `aria-describedby`, no `role="alert"`), so screen reader users can't tell which field failed or why. [mintviz.usv.ro/publications/2025.W4A.3.pdf, pedalpoint.com/2026/05/ai-generated-code-is-inaccessible-by-default/]

**30. Undersized functional text** — Functional text (links, labels, table cells) under ~11px, and body text under ~12px, hurting readability especially on high-DPI screens. [impeccable.style/slop/]

**31. All-caps body text** — Long passages of body copy set in all-uppercase, removing word-shape cues and hurting readability. [impeccable.style/slop/]

**32. Line length too long** — Body text lines wider than ~80 characters with no max-width constraint, making it hard to track back to the next line. [impeccable.style/slop/]

**33. Justified text without hyphenation** — Body text set to justify without hyphenation, producing uneven "rivers" of white space since browsers hyphenate poorly by default. [impeccable.style/slop/]

**34. Tight line height** — Line height set below ~1.3x font size, making multi-line body text feel crammed. [impeccable.style/slop/]

**35. Theme toggle that breaks one mode** — A light/dark mode toggle ships but one mode has broken base styles, fonts, contrast, or layout, having only been verified in the default mode. [github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop]

**36. Generic spinner instead of skeleton screen** — Loading feedback is a bare spinner rather than a skeleton matching the loaded layout, or any contextual loading state. [github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills]

**37. Missing skip-to-content link** — No "skip to main content" link for keyboard users to bypass repeated navigation; found in 0% of accessibility-agnostic generations vs. 100% when WCAG compliance was explicitly requested. [mintviz.usv.ro/publications/2025.W4A.3.pdf, blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/]

**38. Dynamic/status content changes without aria-live announcement** — Content that updates at runtime (balances, notifications, confirmation/status messages) is swapped into plain elements with no `aria-live`/`role="status"` wiring, so screen readers never announce the change. [mintviz.usv.ro/publications/2025.W4A.3.pdf, pedalpoint.com/2026/05/ai-generated-code-is-inaccessible-by-default/, blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/]

**39. Live region over-announcing** — An ARIA live region fires too frequently or at the wrong times, repeatedly interrupting screen reader speech instead of announcing changes sparingly. [pedalpoint.com/2026/05/ai-generated-code-is-inaccessible-by-default/]

**40. Mismatched aria-label vs. visible text** — An element's `aria-label` differs from its visible on-screen text, so voice-control/screen-reader users hear or target text that doesn't match what's displayed. [mintviz.usv.ro/publications/2025.W4A.3.pdf]

**41. Missing page-language declaration** — The document has no (or an incorrect) `lang` attribute on `<html>`, the single most common automated-testing failure across models and prompt types. [mintviz.usv.ro/publications/2025.W4A.3.pdf]

**42. Unclear tab order despite a skip link** — Even when a skip link exists, focus order isn't deliberately managed, so keyboard users can't predict where focus lands next. [mintviz.usv.ro/publications/2025.W4A.3.pdf]

**43. Test-ID selector priority over role/label queries** — Generated test code defaults to `data-testid` selectors instead of Testing Library's recommended role/label/text priority, which also reflects real accessibility semantics. [howtotestfrontend.com/resources/frontend-ai-generated-test-code-smells]

**44. Non-descriptive link text** — Link text reading only "Learn more," "Click here," or "Read more" with no destination context, meaningless to screen reader users navigating by link list. [github.com/alexconner-79/ill-communication]

**45. Icon meaning offloaded to surrounding text** — A decorative/ambiguous icon (e.g. a sparkle) doesn't communicate its function alone; comprehension appears only once paired with adjacent labels or text. [design.google/library/ai-sparkle-icon-research-pozos-schmidt]

**46. State conveyed only via plain nested text, not exposed as accessible state** — E.g. a cart button shows a count ("Basket (3)") as plain nested span text with no `aria-label`/description conveying it as a distinct piece of state, so assistive tech announces it ambiguously. [www.deque.com/blog/vibe-fixing-how-to-validate-ai-generated-code-and-achieve-accessibility-at-the-speed-of-ai/]

**47. Semantic HTML avoidance (div soup)** — Generic `<div>`/`<span>` used broadly in place of appropriate semantic elements (`<table>`, `<ul>`, `<dl>`, `<dialog>`, `<nav>`, `<button>`, `<a>`, `<header>`, `<main>`, `<article>`), losing built-in accessibility and structure. [github.com/Vanszs/Anti-AI-UI, rocketdev.pro/blog/mozhet-li-neyroset-sozdat-kachestvennyy-sayt]

**48. Sound-on autoplay video without muted attribute** — A hero video with `autoplay` but missing `muted`, hostile to users and an accessibility/SEO failure even though browsers usually block it anyway. [github.com/nutlope/hallmark]

**49. Hover-only affordances** — Interactive functionality (menus, delete buttons, tooltips with essential info) that only appears on hover, leaving touch/keyboard users with no way to access it. [github.com/nutlope/hallmark]

**50. Wrap-to-two-lines clickable text** — A button, nav link, or CTA label that wraps onto two lines at narrow viewport widths, breaking scanability and signaling the layout wasn't tested responsively. [github.com/nutlope/hallmark]

**51. Auto-rotating carousel with no pause** — A carousel that auto-advances with no way to pause it, a direct WCAG 2.2.2 (pause, stop, hide) failure. [github.com/nutlope/hallmark]

**52. Equal hover/focus tooltip delay** — Tooltips use the same ~800ms delay for both mouse hover and keyboard focus, when focus should appear instantly since a keyboard user has already committed intent by tabbing there. [github.com/nutlope/hallmark]

**53. Animated focus ring appearance** — A focus outline/box-shadow fades in over ~200ms instead of appearing instantly, leaving keyboard users briefly without a visible indicator when they tab to an element. [github.com/nutlope/hallmark]

## Performance tells

1. **Blanket `transition: all` / `transition-all`** — Every hover/interactive state uses a catch-all transition on all CSS properties instead of specifying which properties to animate — a lazy, wasteful shortcut typical of AI-written CSS. (https://github.com/Krirox/anti-ai-slop-skills; https://habr.com/ru/articles/1004960/)

2. **Animating layout properties instead of transform/opacity** — Animations drive width, height, padding, or margin directly, forcing layout recalculation and jank, instead of using GPU-friendly transform/opacity. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)

3. **Generically bloated/unoptimized code hurting Core Web Vitals** — AI-generated output tends to ship more CSS/JS and unoptimized assets than necessary, measurably dragging down load performance and Core Web Vitals / PageSpeed Insights scores. (https://www.sinton.agency/blog/how-to-spot-a-vibe-coded-website; https://vibe-eval.com/ai-security/vibe-coding-vulnerabilities/)

4. **Unoptimized hero images** — Hero sections use large, uncompressed images that were never resized or compressed, creating an obvious page-weight bottleneck. (https://dev.to/kaplich/i-analyzed-100-vibe-coded-websites-and-found-these-common-mistakes-5275)

5. **LCP over 2.5s** — Largest Contentful Paint exceeding the 2.5-second good threshold, a directly measurable red flag common on template-generated sites. (https://rovela.ai/blog/do-ai-websites-look-generic)

6. **Plugin/widget bloat from template builders** — Stacking many third-party plugins and widgets drags down load times, visible as excessive third-party scripts and slow network waterfalls. (https://rovela.ai/blog/do-ai-websites-look-generic)

7. **Arbitrary test timeouts masking flakiness** — Explicit, unneeded timeout/wait values are added to tests to paper over race conditions instead of fixing the underlying async handling. (https://howtotestfrontend.com/resources/frontend-ai-generated-test-code-smells)

8. **Redundant parallel API fan-out with no consolidation** — Each UI widget independently fetches its own data on load instead of sharing requests or caching, producing many simultaneous calls (including duplicate hits to the same endpoint). (https://dev.to/jsgurujobs/i-reviewed-6-vibe-coded-codebases-here-is-what-i-found-m0o)

9. **Unnecessary client-side rendering** — Pages default to CSR even when static/SSR would suffice, leaving crawlers (and the initial paint) an empty or partially filled DOM before JS executes. (https://rocketdev.pro/blog/mozhet-li-neyroset-sozdat-kachestvennyy-sayt)

10. **Whole-library animation imports instead of lightweight entry points** — Full animation packages (e.g. the entire Framer Motion library) are imported instead of their smaller tree-shaken subsets (LazyMotion, useAnimate), bloating bundle size for no reason. (https://github.com/wilwaldon/Claude-Code-Frontend-Design-Toolkit)

11. **No Core Web Vitals budget in generation** — LCP/INP/CLS thresholds aren't checked against generated pages; performance/SEO auditing is treated as an optional afterthought rather than part of initial generation. (https://github.com/wilwaldon/Claude-Code-Frontend-Design-Toolkit)

12. **`loading="lazy"` applied to the LCP element itself** — The hero image/video that is the page's actual Largest Contentful Paint element is lazy-loaded, delaying its own download and roughly doubling measured LCP versus preloading it. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)

13. **Lottie JSON used for a trivial animation** — A community Lottie animation (spinner, checkmark, loading dots) is dropped in for an effect plain CSS or a hand-built SVG would handle, adding 50-500KB plus runtime cost. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)

14. **Three.js/WebGL for a passive, non-interactive hero object** — A 3D object rendered via Three.js just rotates with no user interaction, costing 100-300KB and GPU overhead for something a static image or SVG could replace. (https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)

15. **Decorative template animations applied everywhere without purpose** — Fade Up, Fade In, Floating, Scale, and Bounce effects are slapped onto nearly every element purely for decoration, with no UX rationale. (https://github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop)

## Cross-cutting / process tells

1. **Assumed logos/avatars without instruction** — App logos, user avatars, or profile photos generated purely on assumption, without explicit instruction or confirmation from the requester/owner. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop)
2. **No distinct visual identity** — Design carries no specific identity: swapping out the logo and product name would leave it feeling interchangeable with any competitor. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop; https://github.com/Rosmarinusofficinalispoloneck995/anti-slop)
3. **Unrequested clone of a popular product** — Overall visual language mimics Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Notion, or another well-known product without the user having asked for that specific look, a known bias from training-data dominance. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop)
4. **Undeclared/inconsistent design "dials"** — Output has no explicit stance on energy, rhythm, or motion — e.g. claims a "bold, varied, cinematic" feel via copy but renders with uniform, static, low-motion sections — a mismatch between stated intent and actual visual behavior. (https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop)
5. **Font outside documented system** — A font family used on the page falls outside the fonts documented in the project's design system (e.g. DESIGN.md). (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
6. **Color outside documented system** — A literal color value used in code falls outside the documented palette instead of using a token. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
7. **Radius outside documented system** — A corner radius value falls outside the documented shape scale. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
8. **Font size outside documented system** — A font size falls between documented type steps rather than matching an approved scale value. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
9. **Broken or placeholder image** — `<img>` tags shipped with empty, missing, or placeholder `src` values, rendering as visibly broken image boxes. (https://impeccable.style/slop/)
10. **Convergence on "Space Grotesk" as the safe distinctive font, even under explicit anti-generic instruction** — Even when told to avoid generic fonts, models still repeatedly converge on the same small set of "distinctive-but-common" fallback choices (most often Space Grotesk) across independent generations, revealing statistical-average behavior underneath a thin layer of variety. (https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website; https://docs.bswen.com/blog/2026-03-20-ai-generated-ui-anti-patterns/)
11. **Animation spam (fade-ins, hover-grow, scrolljacking)** — Uniform decorative motion bolted onto every section: repeated Framer Motion boilerplate (`initial={{opacity:0,y:20}}`, `whileInView`, `whileHover={{scale:1.05}}`), `data-aos="fade-up"` on every section, `hover:scale-105` on every card, or scrolljacking libraries wired to the whole page. (https://www.claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui)
12. **Missing SEO/AEO basics** — No meta descriptions, no Open Graph images, duplicate page titles across routes, absent structured data (e.g. JSON-LD schema), and a thin/flat heading structure (no proper h1-h2 nesting) — signs the site shipped without an SEO pass. (https://www.sinton.agency/blog/how-to-spot-a-vibe-coded-website; https://variantsystems.io/blog/vibe-code-anti-patterns)
13. **Elevated bug and vulnerability rate** — A December 2025 analysis of 470 pull requests found AI-generated code had 1.7x more issues and 2.74x more security vulnerabilities than human-written code, indicating systematically lower code quality/security rigor. (https://smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop)
14. **Missing proof/trust signals** — No testimonials, case studies, or other trust signals placed at the points in the page where they would actually influence a decision. (https://solodesign.cc/blog/ai-design-slop-the-tells/)
15. **Insufficient AI review of test quality** — AI-driven pull-request reviews fail to flag missing tests or poorly written test code, so test-quality gaps pass review unnoticed. (https://howtotestfrontend.com/resources/frontend-ai-generated-test-code-smells)
16. **Accessibility only appears when explicitly prompted** — The same agent produces an inaccessible component (e.g. a modal missing roles, `aria-modal`, `aria-label`, a real `<button>`) by default, but adds proper accessibility markup the moment the word "accessible" is added to the prompt — accessibility is opt-in rather than a baseline default. (https://blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/)
17. **Rationalized justification for an outdated pattern under questioning** — When asked why it avoided a modern primitive (e.g. `<dialog>`), the agent lists weak/outdated justifications (older-browser support, "legacy code or frameworks", need for custom styling) that don't hold up, then reverses itself once pushed further — a sign the original choice was pattern-matching, not reasoned engineering. (https://blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/)
18. **Sycophantic reversal under pushback** — The agent changes its technical recommendation simply in response to continued questioning, rather than holding a consistent position it can defend. (https://blog.logrocket.com/ai-has-an-accessibility-problem/)
19. **Same fade-in-up scroll animation everywhere** — Motion/interaction design defaults to the identical "fade-in-up" reveal animation on scroll for every element, rather than crafted or staggered micro-interactions. (https://www.aiagentskills.ai/blog/ai-websites-all-look-the-same)
20. **Unlabeled dark patterns left unflagged** — Confirmshaming, pre-ticked consent checkboxes, and weighted/asymmetric opt-in button hierarchies appear in a generated flow without being flagged as manipulative. (https://github.com/alexconner-79/ill-communication)
21. **Unescaped user input (XSS gaps)** — User-supplied data is rendered without proper escaping/sanitization, leaving the page open to XSS injection. (https://rocketdev.pro/blog/mozhet-li-neyroset-sozdat-kachestvennyy-sayt)
22. **Linear/default ease animation curves** — Animations use predictable default ease-in-out timing functions rather than custom-tuned easing, making motion feel generic and mechanical rather than purposeful. (https://cloud.tencent.com/developer/article/2687093)
23. **Conditional renders without exit/enter animation wrapper** — UI elements that mount/unmount conditionally (modals, toasts, list items) are swapped instantly with no transition wrapper (e.g. Framer Motion's `AnimatePresence`), even though the interaction calls for one. (https://github.com/wilwaldon/Claude-Code-Frontend-Design-Toolkit)
24. **Dynamic style changes without transitions** — Properties that change dynamically (color, size, position) snap instantly instead of using a CSS transition or animation. (https://github.com/wilwaldon/Claude-Code-Frontend-Design-Toolkit)
